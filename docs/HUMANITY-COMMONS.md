# Commons of Care

This seventh outdoor place adds a human question to the mathematical city: how
should people decide together when the available resources do not meet every
reported need? It is an original fictional thought experiment, not a historical
simulation, real household dataset, diagnosis or claim about a best ethical rule.

The agreed civic anchor is upper-city (−90,190), radius10 m, facing +Z, clear of
the park, pond, Proof Atelier and existing houses. `humanity-commons-model.js`
exports `HUMANITY_COMMONS` and `COMMONS_PATH` as the shared authority. The 3.4 m
spur runs from the public approach (−90,204) to the real honeycomb junction
(−79.67433714816835,230). The city layout owns paving connections, wall openings,
tree exclusion and the map; the Commons does not invent a separate route.

An ivory sixfold shade covers one round table. Three abstract stone forms and
garden benches frame the conversation; the figures are original procedural
sculptures, not extra voiced characters or copied meshes. Three water columns
change height when a visitor changes a rule, beside fixed reported-need gauges.
All architecture stays inside the ten-metre footprint and the front approach
remains clear. No external texture, model, source image, recording, reflection
pass or new light is used.

Six equal indivisible units and needs [1,2,5] give three distinct outcomes:

| Rule | Allocations A/B/C | Unmet A/B/C | Above reported need |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Equal shares | 2/2/2 | 0/0/3 | 1/0/0 |
| Greatest remaining shortfall first | 1/1/4 | 0/1/1 | 0/0/0 |
| One-unit basic minimum, then complete the smallest remaining need | 1/2/3 | 0/0/2 | 0/0/0 |

The greatest-shortfall rule allocates one unit at a time to the largest remaining
need, breaking ties A/B/C. The minimum rule first gives one unit to each positive
reported need, then completes the smallest positive shortfall first, with the
same tie order. Generic model inputs retain unallocated reserve once all needs
are met; allocations plus reserve always equal the budget. Equal allocation can
exceed a reported need, which is shown explicitly rather than hidden.

The English reader identifies its assumptions: reported needs are fixed and
sincere, letters carry no moral priority, and units cannot be divided. There is
no correct-ethics score. It asks who was absent when needs were described, and
states that real care also involves listening and consent. These original
questions do not depend on unverified empirical claims or copied source prose.

`installHumanityCommons(M,T)` returns `M.humanityCommons` with `visit`, `open`,
`interact`, `near`, `state` and `dispose`. The public arrival stays on the actual
approach, so walk forward to the sign. Opening and changing the model require
the same ground height, city mode and physical proximity; readers/maps/hidden
pages block opening. The optional nearby button sits at the top, clear of mobile
movement arrows; native dialog Close/Escape restores walking. The main city
installs its existing E/A chain. The physical table, supports, planters, figures
and benches use matching collision checks.

There are no automatic saves on arrival, reading or changing allocations.
Only **Carry this question** calls the existing visitor-memory
`store.question('human-care','personal',CARE_QUESTION)`. It preserves the stable
question and any previous personal argument. The module never reads another
storage system, switches accounts, records a visitor or writes an answer.

`node tests/humanity-commons.mjs` independently enumerates six-unit allocations,
checks 375 resource-conservation cases and the three exact outcomes, checks the
clear real approach and model/3D bowl agreement, exercises physical and modal
gates, proves notebook access happens only on the explicit button, and verifies
owned geometry/material/texture/UI cleanup and restored host hooks.
