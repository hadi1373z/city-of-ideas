# Two outer houses become working investigations

Chomsky’s and Ada Lovelace’s existing lots, rotated entrances, windows and roofs
remain in place. The other four first-study houses remain available. The separate
House of Psychoanalysis is untouched and still design-only. New teaching copy
and controls are English, while existing language preferences remain respected.

## Chomsky

The decorative relief is replaced by a live labelled wooden grammar tree. Visitors
generate up to five a/b pairs with S → aSb | ε, then step an explicit stack scanner
on custom strings of at most twelve symbols. The grammar’s sample and the tested
input are labelled separately. A supported memory bench reflects actual stack
height. Empty input, phase changes, leftover markers and premature pops are handled.

The second investigation enumerates every binary parse of expressions with up to
six single digits and +/*. Precedence is not silently supplied. Switching to an
explicit precedence grammar yields its unique left-associative parse. Each tree’s
shape, fully parenthesised expression and value are visible. Equal values do not
collapse distinct trees. English problems develop induction, invariants, a
finite-state indistinguishability argument, ambiguity versus inherent ambiguity,
and the limitations of the toy-language analogy.

## Ada Lovelace

The original wheel remains beside a new, supported four-register bench. An
addition updates one register at a time; Crank finishes the current row. The
visible n and partial-row indicator prevent an intermediate value from being
misrepresented as a complete next sample. Squares, cubes, triangular numbers,
four powers of two and four custom integer observations initialise the machine.
The difference table and Newton continuation agree with the addition process.

A second live plot and value table compare the cubic p with
q(n)=p(n)+k·n(n−1)(n−2)(n−3), for integer k from −2 to 2. Both rules agree on
all four observations but can disagree on the next value. The 1,2,4,8 example
distinguishes the cubic continuation 15 from exponential continuation 16. Worked
questions ask for the invariant, update order, interpolation uniqueness under a
degree bound and the factorisation of all polynomial countermodels.

Text distinguishes Babbage’s Difference and Analytical Engines, and relates the
mechanism to Lovelace’s discussion of operations. The exhibit is an original
teaching construction; it is not her Bernoulli program or a mechanical replica.
Primary links include Chomsky’s 1956 paper, museum descriptions of the engines,
and the York University transcription of Lovelace’s 1843 notes.

## Integration and persistence

Room interaction, exhibit picks, house-directory controls and the context action
open the investigations. Entering from outside is explicit. Face the exhibit
changes camera direction without moving the visitor; the cross-house Visit
button names its destination. New tables are solid and the original central
entrance lines remain clear. Drawing resources and borrowed original features
are restored on disposal.

`city-of-ideas:house-investigations:v1` is a new independent local key. On reload,
derived scanner and engine state is reconstructed by replaying validated inputs
and operation counts. First conjectures retain their original context and cannot
be overwritten by later notes. Draft text is saved only by the explicit Keep
buttons; observation saves also use the existing notebook. Previous study notes,
voices, assets, credits and account connections remain untouched.

Checks cover all 8,191 a/b strings through length twelve, expression parse counts
and precedence, 625 cubic families through twelve rows, partial arithmetic states,
countermodels, corrupt saves, explicit private writes, physical controls, geometry,
doorway/aisle clearance, solid furniture and cleanup. Browser checks separately
inspect both rooms and the compact layout.
